SarunasVerner

Theoretical physicist, KICP Fellow at the University of Chicago

I am a theoretical cosmologist and particle physicist. I study inflation and reheating and how dark matter, axions and other new particles were produced in the early universe, including in phase transitions and black hole evaporation. I confront these models with measurements of the cosmic microwave background and with gravitational wave and astrophysical data, and I use machine learning to find structure in physical theories.

About

I joined the Kavli Institute for Cosmological Physics at the University of Chicago as a KICP Fellow in 2025, after postdoctoral research at the University of Florida and a Ph.D. at the University of Minnesota with Keith Olive.

I was born in Vilnius, Lithuania, trained in physics and astronomy at the University of Southampton, and have mentored students on projects from cosmological particle production to machine-learned symmetries.
