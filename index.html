SarunasVerner

Theoretical physicist, KICP Fellow at the University of Chicago

I work where cosmology meets particle physics: how inflation and reheating produce dark matter, how new physics leaves gravitational-wave and astrophysical signatures, and how machine learning can reveal the structure of physical theories.

From fundamental theory to observable physics

My work connects the particle content of a theory, its evolution in the early universe, and the signals that can test it.

Selected work

One paper from each direction. The full record has 49 papers and preprints since 2015, four of them single-author.

  • Early universe

    Gravitational production of massive spin-2 particles during reheating

    S. Verner

    Physical Review D 113, 123503 (2026), arXiv:2512.19041

    A purely gravitational route to spin-2 dark matter during reheating, in which the longitudinal mode dominates production.

  • Particle physics

    The electroweak sphaleron revisited, I and II

    K. T. Matchev and S. Verner

    Physical Review D 112, 113009 and 113010 (2025), arXiv:2505.05607 and 2505.05608

    From unstable field configurations to their nonlinear decay dynamics, with a public numerical implementation.

  • Gravitational waves & probes

    New method for the astrometric direct detection of ultralight dark matter

    J. A. Dror and S. Verner

    Physical Review Letters 134, 111003 (2025), arXiv:2406.03526

    A proposal to search for ultralight dark matter through correlated changes in the apparent positions of distant sources.

  • Machine learning

    Deep learning symmetries and their Lie groups, algebras, and subalgebras from first principles

    R. T. Forestano, K. T. Matchev, K. Matcheva, A. Roman, E. B. Unlu and S. Verner

    Machine Learning: Science and Technology 4, 025027 (2023), arXiv:2301.05638

    A method for learning continuous symmetry transformations and their algebraic structure from labeled data.
