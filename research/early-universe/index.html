Research

Cosmology

Inflation, reheating & dark matter

How did the early universe populate the visible and dark sectors?

The transition from inflation to the hot universe connects high-energy particle physics with the thermal history of the cosmos. I study the production of particles during inflation, preheating and reheating, including situations where gravity provides the only connection between the inflaton, the Standard Model and dark matter.

My work follows particle production across different masses, spins and couplings, using analytical approximations and numerical evolution. The goal is to determine which mechanisms produce the observed abundance, which are excluded by cosmological perturbations or structure formation, and how these conclusions depend on the inflationary and reheating history.

Selected contributions

Questions, calculations and results.

  • Gravitational portals

    I led a collaborative study of graviton-mediated production from both the inflaton condensate and the thermal bath. Comparing these channels reveals when a gravitational contribution controls the abundance and when it affects the early radiation history.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2112.15214

  • Nonminimal superheavy dark matter

    In my single-author study, I combined analytical and numerical calculations to examine how curvature couplings change the production of superheavy scalar dark matter. The analysis includes abundance and isocurvature constraints, evaluated in a specified inflationary background.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2408.11889

  • Inflation confronted with data

    I led a collaborative study of how CMB measurements restrict attractor inflationary models and their reheating histories, using constraints from Planck, BICEP/Keck, ACT DR6 and SPT-3G.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2510.18656

  • Axions and higher-spin particles

    My work also connects axion kinetic misalignment and parametric resonance to inflation, and studies gravitational production for particles with different spins, including massive spin-2 fields.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2004.00629

Where this leads

I am extending these questions to multifield and supergravity settings, and studying axion populations from early-universe processes. Work in preparation includes a review of reheating and studies of the cosmic axion background and Hawking axions.
