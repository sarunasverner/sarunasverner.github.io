How can weakly interacting particles reveal themselves through gravity?

Particles that are difficult to access in the laboratory can still influence the universe through their gravitational effects. I develop cosmological and astrophysical probes of new physics, focusing on signals from spectator scalar fields, cosmic strings and ultralight dark matter.

The task is to connect a microscopic model to an observable: identify the physical mechanism, calculate the evolution and signal, and establish the assumptions needed to interpret a measurement or a null result. This links particle theory to cosmological perturbations and observational strategy.

The gravitational wave landscapeEach curve marks where a current or future experiment can detect a stochastic gravitational wave background: the region above it. The curves are those compiled in my plotting notebook from published sensitivity studies. Solid lines are current data, and dashed lines are planned or proposed experiments. The violins are the NANOGrav 15-year signal and the short bar is the LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA O3 limit. The shaded band at the top is excluded by the Planck 2018 bound on extra radiation, ΔNeff.

Selected contributions

Questions, calculations and results.

  • Spectator fields and gravitational waves

    With collaborators, I study how fluctuations of spectator scalar fields evolve and source gravitational wave backgrounds. The calculations connect particle production, reheating and the evolution of perturbations.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2512.04240

  • Astrometric searches for ultralight dark matter

    I led a project with Jeff Dror proposing the use of apparent positions of distant sources to probe ultralight dark matter. The work identifies secular and annually modulating signatures and estimates the reach of astrometric observations.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2406.03526

  • Cosmic strings and their loops

    Recent work investigates gravitational waves from long strings and loops, extending my program of early-universe sources and their signatures.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2609.22430

  • Matter perturbations of spectator fields

    A complementary line of work examines gravitational waves generated by matter perturbations of spectator scalars, broadening the connection between dark-sector dynamics and gravitational wave observables.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2604.05078

Where this leads

A central next step is to connect theoretical signatures more closely to realistic observational analyses, keeping the relevant backgrounds, assumptions and parameter dependence explicit.
