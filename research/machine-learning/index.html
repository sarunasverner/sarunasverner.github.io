Research

Methods

Machine learning for theoretical physics

Can algorithms discover the structures that organize physical theories?

I develop machine-learning methods for questions usually addressed through analytical reasoning: identifying symmetries, determining their algebraic structure and searching for theoretical models with desired properties. This work sits at the intersection of particle physics, mathematics and scientific machine learning.

The emphasis is on interpretable structure. The methods aim to recover generators, relations or model parameters that can be inspected and used in further theoretical work. Controlled examples provide a way to test what an algorithm has actually learned.

The root system of E6The 72 roots of the exceptional Lie algebra E6, projected onto its Coxeter plane, with an edge between every pair of roots whose inner product is one. E6 is among the groups recovered from data in our work on machine-learned symmetries.

Selected contributions

Questions, calculations and results.

  • Discovering continuous symmetries

    I led a collaborative project developing neural-network methods that learn transformations preserving labeled data, with constraints enforcing the structure of symmetry generators and closed algebras.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2301.05638

  • Efficient and sparse representations

    Related studies examine sparse representations, faster discovery algorithms and the group-theoretic structure of learned symmetries, including the exceptional Lie groups G2, F4 and E6.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2307.04891

  • Exploring theory landscapes

    Using the quark Yukawa sector as a controlled example, we constructed loss functions that combine agreement with data and explicit structural criteria such as sparsity, uniformity or symmetry.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2401.11513

  • From learned transformations to group structure

    A further study asks how to identify the algebraic structure of machine-learned symmetries, connecting numerical discovery with conventional group-theoretic descriptions.

    Read the paper, arXiv:2309.07860

Where this leads

Current work in preparation includes AI agents for particle physics, extending my interest in computational methods that support theoretical reasoning and discovery.
